E-commerce

Buying and selling through digital channels. Behind every product page is a connected system of payments, inventory, delivery, and customer care.

01 / The customer journey

From discovery to delivery

Each step connects the storefront to the systems that fulfill the order.

  1. 01

    Discover

    The customer finds a product through search, social media, or a marketplace.

  2. 02

    Choose

    They compare details, select a variant, and add the product to a cart.

  3. 03

    Pay

    Checkout collects delivery details and processes payment securely.

  4. 04

    Fulfill

    The seller confirms stock, packs the order, and hands it to a carrier.

  5. 05

    Support

    Tracking, returns, refunds, and customer service continue after purchase.

02 / Business models

Who sells to whom?

The relationship between buyer and seller shapes pricing, order size, and the buying experience.

Common e-commerce models
ModelRelationshipExampleTypical priority
B2CBusiness to consumerA retailer sells headphones to a shopper.Convenient checkout and delivery
B2BBusiness to businessA wholesaler supplies packaging to retailers.Bulk pricing, invoices, and repeat orders
C2CConsumer to consumerAn individual sells a used watch through a marketplace.Seller reputation and buyer protection
D2CBrand directly to consumerA shoe brand sells through its own online store.Brand experience and customer relationships

D2C is a form of B2C that describes a brand's direct sales channel.

03 / Behind the storefront

Choose a fulfillment approach

Fulfillment affects inventory control, delivery speed, operating costs, and returns.

Fulfillment options and trade-offs
ApproachStock & shippingMain benefitTrade-off
In-houseThe seller stores, packs, and ships products.Direct control of packaging and qualityRequires space, staff, and inventory investment
Third-party logisticsA logistics partner stores and ships the seller's inventory.Outsourced warehouse operationsService fees and dependence on a partner
DropshippingA supplier ships directly to the customer.Less inventory held by the sellerLess control over stock, packaging, and delivery
Digital deliveryA file, license, or subscription is delivered online.No physical packing or shippingRequires reliable access and license management
